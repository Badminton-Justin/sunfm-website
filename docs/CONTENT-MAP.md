# Sun FM — Content Map

**Purpose:** this is the information-architecture reference for the blog. Read it before writing a new post, so topic placement and interlinking are a deliberate decision instead of something an audit discovers later. Update it after publishing anything that changes the picture below (new post, new cluster, a cluster that's outgrown its shape).

This is not a substitute for the SERP/gap research in `/new-blog` Step 1 — it's the *internal* map (what exists, how it's grouped, how it should link) that complements the *external* research (what's ranking, what's missing).

---

## Route structure

- `/<category>/<slug>` — blog posts. Categories: `training`, `nutrition`, `wellness`.
- `/blog` — full post index (all categories, `Related Articles`-free, just the grid).
- `/<category>` — per-category index page.
- `/<city>-personal-trainer` — 10 city landing pages (San Jose, Sunnyvale, Cupertino, Santa Clara, Mountain View, Los Gatos, Saratoga, Los Altos, Milpitas, Campbell), driven by `src/lib/service-areas.ts`.
- `/tools/movement-screen` — the primary conversion asset. Nearly every blog post should route toward this or `/#apply` in its closing section.
- `/team`, `/start` (PPC-only, noindexed), `/#apply`.

- `push-up-test-at-home-what-your-number-means-after-40` (2026-08-13) — fills the strength-relative-to-bodyweight axis. The decomposition is the point: a push-up count is pressing strength plus trunk stability plus shoulder position, and the post sorts a low number into which of the three ran out (via an incline push-up check, a plank hold, and a slow-negative elbow/scapular check), then gives a six-week plan per cause. Anchored on Yang et al., JAMA Netw Open 2019 (1,104 male career firefighters, mean age 39.6, 37 CVD events over 8,601 person-years), and the post spends real space on why the headline "96 percent" is shakier than it reads: wide CI on the top band, a non-monotonic dose-response (21-30 beat 31-40), and the authors' own statement that it may not generalize to women, older adults, or non-active people. Norms are the standard full push-up tables, collapsed from seven bands to three. Links out to `core-stability-test-at-home` and `shoulder-mobility-test-at-home`, each attached to a specific failure cause rather than dropped in as related reading. Inbound link from `grip-strength-test-at-home-build-after-40` at its "where to start" close, framed as the second upper-body number to sit next to the dead hang.
- `muscle-power-test-at-home-after-40` (2026-09-21) — reopens the longevity-marker sub-thread that the 2026-08-13 note declared closed, on a genuinely new axis: **rate of force development**. The four existing markers measure mobility (sitting-rising), balance, aerobic capacity (cardio test), and strength endurance (push-up test), and the sarcopenia post adds mass; none of them measure how *fast* force is produced, which the research says declines earlier and faster than strength. Three tests: five-rep sit-to-stand run through the Alcazar power equation (Alcazar et al., *Exp Gerontol* 2018, 40 adults 70-87 validated against leg-press transducer, r = 0.72, plus 1,804 in the Toledo cohort; the 2021 *JCSM* normative paper, 9,320 older adults across four countries, supplies the 2.6/2.1 W/kg low-power cutoffs, the 3.55/2.85 W/kg older-adult medians, and the 0.42/0.33 W/kg MCIDs), a single-flight stair-climb power test (Bean et al., *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 2007, 138 mobility-limited adults mean age 75, r = 0.47-0.52 vs leg press, only power measure predicting standing balance), and a wall jump-and-reach gated behind passing the first two (Sayers et al., *MSSE* 1999 equation, 108 college-age adults, stated as a tracking number only). Under-60 pass/borderline/fail bands are explicitly the author's training lines extrapolated from Alcazar's decline rates, since every published cutoff was built in people over 60. Framing anchors: Skelton et al., *Age Ageing* 1994 (100 adults 65-89, power -3.5%/yr vs strength -1-2%/yr) and Runge et al., *Clin Physiol Funct Imaging* 2004 (258 active adults 18-88, jump power >50% lower at 80 than 20 with no change in calf cross-section). Training section runs on Tschopp et al., *Age Ageing* 2011 meta-analysis (11 RCTs, 377 adults 60+, pooled ES 0.32 for power vs conventional training, safety inconclusive), presented as a small advantage per the movement-positive rule, with an explicit paragraph that keeping a heavy-and-slow program is a defensible choice. **Deliberate non-overlaps:** the 5xSTS is the same movement as the sarcopenia post's Test 3 and the post says so up front (same chair, different question: seconds vs watts), and the single-flight stair test is distinguished from the cardio post's four-flight aerobic climb in-text. Decomposition sorts a low score into strength deficit, slow rate of force development, ankle/hip range, or balance/confidence, each routed to an existing post. Links out to `sarcopenia-self-test-at-home-am-i-losing-muscle-after-50`, `cardio-fitness-test-at-home-after-40`, `ankle-mobility-test-at-home`, `balance-test-at-home-single-leg-checks-over-40`, `strength-training-for-longevity-beginners-guide-over-30`, and `strength-training-with-knee-osteoarthritis-after-50`; inbound link added from the sarcopenia post's Test 3 paragraph, which already said "power drops faster with age than strength does" and had nowhere to send the reader. SERP differentiator: Outside, Men's Journal, Men's Fitness, and Eat This all explain that power declines first and stop; topendsports has a jump calculator with no age framing; nobody scores, decomposes, or programs. **Longevity-marker sub-thread is now five posts** (mobility, balance, aerobic, strength endurance, power) plus the sarcopenia mass screen. Treat it as closed again unless new research opens another axis; reactive strength / ground-contact time was considered and rejected as a sixth because it needs a force plate or a contact mat to measure honestly.
- `glute-activation-test-at-home-dead-butt-syndrome` (2026-09-18) — tests whether the glutes *fire* (single-leg bridge hold at a 135° knee angle, prone hip extension firing order, single-leg stance pelvic drop), which no post had measured. - `tennis-elbow-vs-golfers-elbow-self-test` (2026-09-03) — claims the elbow/forearm, which the 2026-09 audit confirmed was the last unclaimed segment of the kinetic chain (ankle, hip, thoracic spine, hamstring, core, grip, lumbar spine, balance, foot arch, shoulder x3, knee, neck, posture, SI joint were all taken). Sits in 1c rather than 1a because the job is differential diagnosis, not range of motion: it sorts lateral from medial and then decomposes the result into which of four causes owns it (tendon capacity, accumulated desk load, upstream shoulder/thoracic contribution, technique and equipment). Scoring backbone is Dorf et al., J Hand Surg Am 2007 (81 patients; grip in the affected arm 50% of the healthy side with the elbow extended vs 69% flexed, an 8% flexion-to-extension drop ~80% sensitive and 85% specific), stated with its limits since it was a single-centre retrospective review of already-diagnosed patients and covered the lateral side only. Other anchors: Shiri et al., Am J Epidemiol 2006 (4,783 Finns 30-64; lateral 1.3% vs medial 0.4%, repetitive-plus-forceful work OR 5.6) to kill the "you must play tennis" assumption, Bisset et al. BMJ 2006 (198 adults, 52 weeks; injection best at 6 weeks, then 47 of 65 successes relapsed) with Coombes et al. JAMA 2013 (165 adults) replicating it, Peterson et al. Clin Rehabil 2014 (120 patients; eccentric beat concentric by ~10% absolute pain reduction, NNT 10, no functional difference), and Tyler et al. Int J Sports PT 2014 (20 patients, uncontrolled, 70% golfers) as the deliberately weak medial anchor. The injection finding is framed as a horizon trade-off rather than a verdict, per the movement-positive voice rule. SERP differentiator: page one is a topical-cream affiliate, three surgical-referral funnels, a clinician wiki describing two-person tests, and a Healthline overview, none of which score the result or write for a desk worker who also lifts. - `hypermobility-test-at-home-beighton-score-for-lifters` (2026-10-03) — the cluster's **inverted-interpretation** post, and the only one on the site where a high score is a finding rather than an achievement. Every other cluster-1 test asks whether you have enough range; this one asks whether you have more than you can control, which is why it belongs here despite reading against the grain of its 21 neighbours. Uses the Beighton 9-point score as a published instrument rather than a site-invented test: Beighton, Solomon & Soskolne, *Ann Rheum Dis* 1973 (the original articular-mobility survey). Thresholds come from Malfait et al., *Am J Med Genet C* 2017 (the international EDS classification): ≥5 for adults under 50, ≥4 for adults over 50, ≥6 prepubertal — the age banding is given real space because it flips results for the site's 40+ readership. Supporting anchors, both stated with their limits: Pacey et al., *AJSM* 2010 (18 studies; knee injury OR 4.69 **but CI 1.33-16.52**, contact sports only, and no ankle effect — the post says plainly that nobody in the analysis was lifting) and Smith et al., *Rheumatol Int* 2013 (5 studies / 254 people; lower-limb joint position sense and movement-detection threshold both worse at p<0.001, while shoulder JPS did **not** reach significance). Juul-Kristensen et al., *Rheumatology* 2007 (kappa 0.74 across 40 patients, between *trained* examiners) anchors an explicit "your self-scored result is noisier than that" caveat. **Deliberate non-overlaps:** the research recommendation originally proposed a passive-vs-active straight-leg-raise gap test — that is already in `hamstring-flexibility-test-at-home`, which runs AKE as Test 2, SLR as Test 3, and spells out the gap interpretation in prose; dropped. Replacement checks are both new to the site: a standing knee lock-out habit check (can you hold 10° short of lock for 60s/side) and a seated knee joint-position-sense reproduction test filmed on a phone. Beighton item 9 (palms flat on floor) deliberately collides with the hamstring post's toe-touch and the post uses that collision as the teaching point — same movement, opposite meaning. Single-leg balance is a prescribed drill only, never scored, since `balance-test-at-home-single-leg-checks-over-40` owns it. Medical guardrail: dislocations, skin involvement, family history and systemic symptoms are routed to a clinician, and the post states repeatedly that the score is a range measurement and not a diagnosis. Links out to `hamstring-flexibility-test-at-home` and `mobility-vs-strength-training-why-you-need-both`; inbound from that same mobility-vs-strength post, whose "you can be flexible and immobile at the same time" passage was the exact anchor and sat at 6 links. SERP differentiator: the diagnostic query is medical-owned (Cleveland Clinic, the Ehlers-Danlos Society) while the *training* query is fragmented and weak (gmb.io, PureGym blog, scattered PT clinics), and nothing on page one converts the 9-point score into banded changes to range, tempo, and exercise selection. **Note for future audits:** this post makes the site's mobility advice conditional on where a reader sits in the laxity distribution, so new cluster-1 posts should consider whether their pass/fail bands need a hypermobile caveat.

- `exercises-for-bone-density-after-40` (2026-08-25) — seeds a **skeletal-health thread** off the longevity pillar, and the first post on the site to treat bone as its own adaptation target rather than a side effect of lifting. Bone density had exactly two passing mentions across the first 43 posts, both in the longevity pillar. The organizing idea is that bone reads load magnitude and strain rate rather than exercise minutes, and that the signal saturates within a few loading cycles, which is why the whole SERP's advice (walking, yoga, light dumbbells, resistance bands) under-delivers at the hip and spine. Anchors: LIFTMOR (Watson et al., JBMR 2018 — 101 postmenopausal women, mean age 65, T-score < -1.0, 2x/week, 5x5 at >85% 1RM on deadlift/squat/overhead press plus jumping chin-ups, 8 months, +2.9% lumbar spine BMD vs -1.2% control, zero injuries) and the unilateral hopping trial (*Bone*, 2013 — 50 men aged 65-80, up to 50 hops/day on one randomly assigned leg for 12 months, each man his own control). The post spends real space on LIFTMOR's limits, since every ranking page extrapolates a supervised trial in older postmenopausal women to the general reader without saying so. Also covers T-score bands and the Sinaki flexion-vs-extension fracture finding (16% vs 89%), flagged as small and dated but mechanistically plausible. Impact loading gets its own section because both anchor studies included it and the SERP universally omits it. Links out to `deadlift-setup-for-over-30` (in the hinge section) and `strength-training-for-longevity-beginners-guide-over-30` (in the close); inbound link added from the longevity pillar's bone-density sentence, which previously dead-ended. Where this thread could grow: nothing obvious yet — a second post needs either a distinct population (post-fracture return, perimenopause specifically) or a distinct mechanism, since load, impact, and the diagnosis question are all covered here.
- `training-around-a-chronic-injury-that-never-fully-healed` (2026-08-01) — fills the gap between `injury-vs-soreness-when-to-train-through-pain-over-30` (week-one triage) and `personal-trainer-for-injury-recovery-bridge-from-physical-therapy` (formal PT discharge): the population that had neither, just an old ache that quietly stuck around for years. - `strength-training-with-knee-osteoarthritis-after-50` (2026-09-09) — extends cluster 2's "coming back from a setback" thread to a named degenerative diagnosis, which nothing on the site had covered. Distinct from `training-around-a-chronic-injury-that-never-fully-healed` (undiagnosed lingering aches, compensation mapping) and from `knee-pain-when-sitting-at-a-desk` (desk-position pain triage): this one is about a diagnosis people receive and then quit training over. The organizing idea is that the research says two things at once and every ranking page picks one and drops the other, so the post holds both. Anchors: Bricca et al., *BJSM* 2019 (9 RCTs, 14 comparisons, MRI-assessed cartilage; loading exercise seems not harmful, and in the established-OA group six comparisons showed no effect on thickness/volume/defects — with the authors' own low quality-of-evidence rating stated plainly); Messier et al., START, *JAMA* 2021 (377 adults 50+, radiographic OA, 18 months, high vs low intensity vs attention control; adjusted WOMAC pain 5.1 vs 4.9, MCID 2, and knee joint compressive force 2453 N vs 2512 N, both null — plus 53 nonserious adverse events in the high-intensity arm vs 4 in control); and Yan et al., *BMJ* 2025 (network meta-analysis, 217 RCTs, 15,684 participants, 1990-2024; aerobic ranked first with moderate certainty, SMD -1.10 short-term and -1.19 mid-term for pain, SUCRA mean 0.72, while strengthening still produced a large mid-term function gain at SMD 0.86). The synthesis is the differentiator: load doesn't appear to damage the joint, heavy lifting isn't a dependable pain treatment, and aerobic work carries the strongest pain evidence, so the two modalities are doing different jobs. Also covers why "bone on bone" is a joint-space reading that correlates loosely with symptoms, the ≤5/10-settling-within-24-hours pain-monitoring rule (given as a widely used clinical rule of thumb, not attributed to a specific trial), and position/range adjustments (box squats, goblet loading, split squats, leg press) instead of dropping movements. SERP differentiator: page one is raw PubMed and NCBI Bookshelf entries plus two Quora threads and affiliate blogs that directly contradict each other on whether squats help or harm, with nothing written for the reader who just got the diagnosis. Voice note: the Yan result makes it very easy to slip into "cardio beats lifting," and the post deliberately frames it as different jobs for different goals per the movement-positive rule. Links out to `cardio-fitness-test-at-home-after-40` (the aerobic finding needed a landing place) and `knee-pain-when-sitting-at-a-desk`; inbound link added from that same knee-pain post's "when to see a doctor" section, which triaged readers toward a clinician and previously had no route back for the ones whose answer came back as arthritis. **Where this could grow:** hip osteoarthritis was taken on 2026-10-06 (`strength-training-with-hip-osteoarthritis-self-test-after-50`, below).

- `strength-training-with-hip-osteoarthritis-self-test-after-50` (2026-10-06) — the hip sibling to the knee OA post, filling the gap that post flagged. Kept under the 30% overlap line by being built differently: the knee post is a pure evidence read, while this one adds cluster 1's test-score-decompose format (three at-home tests, four profiles, an 8-week program keyed to the profiles). ## Linking philosophy

- **No blanket sibling links.** The automated "Related Articles" block (`getRelatedPosts` in `src/lib/blog.ts`) scores candidates by shared *words* (not whole tag phrases — tags here are unique long-tail keyword strings per post, so exact-tag matching finds almost nothing between clearly related posts) weighted by inverse document frequency, so specific overlap (e.g. "shoulder" + "impingement") counts far more than near-universal words ("desk", "San Jose"). It's automatic and doesn't need per-post maintenance, but it's a relevance signal, not a substitute for judgment — a hand-picked contextual link in prose is always stronger than an algorithmic card grid.
- **Manual inline links stay conservative:** one link where it earns its place, in prose, at a natural anchor. Not a "related posts" dump inside the body. (This is already the `/new-blog` Step 4 rule — this map is what tells you *which* post is the right one to link, before you get there.)
- **Every post should route toward `/tools/movement-screen` and `/#apply`** in its closing section regardless of cluster.

## Before writing a new post

1. Check which cluster (if any) the topic belongs to above.
2. If it's cluster 1 (desk-worker self-check), check the 30%-overlap rule against all 21 existing posts, not just the obvious neighbor.
3. Decide the 1-2 existing posts that should get an inbound link, and which existing post(s) the new one should link out to — based on the cluster map above, not a fresh search each time.
4. After publishing, add the new post to the relevant cluster list in this file (or start a new cluster section if it doesn't fit an existing one).
