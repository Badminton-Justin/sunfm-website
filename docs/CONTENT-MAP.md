# Sun FM — Content Map

**Purpose:** this is the information-architecture reference for the blog. Read it before writing a new post, so topic placement and interlinking are a deliberate decision instead of something an audit discovers later. Update it after publishing anything that changes the picture below (new post, new cluster, a cluster that's outgrown its shape).

This is not a substitute for the SERP/gap research in `/new-blog` Step 1 — it's the *internal* map (what exists, how it's grouped, how it should link) that complements the *external* research (what's ranking, what's missing).

---

## Route structure

- `/<category>/<slug>` — blog posts. Categories: `training`, `nutrition`, `wellness`.
- `/blog` — full post index (all categories, `Related Articles`-free, just the grid).
- `/<category>` — per-category index page.
- `/<city>-personal-trainer` — 10 city landing pages (San Jose, Sunnyvale, Cupertino, Santa Clara, Mountain View, Los Gatos, Saratoga, Los Altos, Milpitas, Campbell), driven by `src/lib/service-areas.ts`.
- `/tools/movement-screen` — the primary conversion asset. Nearly every blog post should route toward this or `/#apply` in its closing section.
- `/team`, `/start` (PPC-only, noindexed), `/#apply`.

### 3. Nutrition (5 posts)

- `protein-intake-for-muscle-after-40`
- `hydration-and-electrolytes-for-strength-training-after-40`
- `meal-prep-for-busy-professionals`
- `lose-fat-without-losing-muscle-after-40` (2026-08-31) — the first post on the site to cover calorie intake or body composition at all. Across the first 46 posts the word "deficit" never appeared: the protein post gave grams-per-pound and stopped, and `how-long-to-see-results-strength-training-after-40` mentioned body composition at the 6-month mark in passing. This fills that hole from the nutrition side. The organizing idea is that a stalled scale is a symptom with four distinct causes, each needing a different response: you're not actually in a deficit, the deficit is steep enough that you're paying in lean mass, you're recomposing and the scale can't resolve two things moving in opposite directions, or recovery (sleep, work stress) is the real constraint and cutting further makes it worse. Applies cluster 1's decompose-the-score format to nutrition, which is what separates it from the SERP: Healthline plus a wall of affiliate blogs (Fit Father Project, hereforvanity, awakeniv, femalefitnesssystems) all deliver the same three bullets and none of them diagnose. Anchors: Longland et al., AJCN 2016 (40 young men, 4 weeks, 40% deficit, 2.4 vs 1.2 g/kg protein, resistance + interval work 6 days/week; high-protein arm lost 4.8 kg fat and gained 1.2 kg lean, low-protein arm lost 3.5 kg fat and held lean flat) and Lichtman et al., NEJM 1992 (self-reported intake underreported ~47%, exercise overreported ~51%). Both are presented with their limits stated plainly, since the Longland cohort was 23-year-old supervised men on a deficit far steeper than anything a working adult should run, and the Lichtman sample was small and specific. Closes with a four-week measure-then-adjust reset. Links out to `protein-intake-for-muscle-after-40`, `how-hard-should-you-be-training-rpe-self-test-after-30` (the too-steep-deficit tells), and `sleep-and-muscle-recovery-for-strength-training-over-30` (cause four); inbound link added from the protein post's "undereating on rest days" section, which was the closest existing anchor and was only 2 outbound links deep.

- `creatine-after-40-what-it-does-and-how-to-tell-if-its-working` (2026-09-03) — took the creatine runner-up flagged in the 2026-08-31 research below, and the first supplement-specific post on the site. Applies cluster 1's decompose-and-self-test format to a supplement, which is the whole differentiator: the SERP (jinfiniti, vitamins40plus, PrimeVital, Three Rivers concierge medicine, BodySpec, ubiehealth) is almost entirely retailers and thin health blogs listing the same benefits, and not one of them answers the reader's actual question, which is whether it's working *for them*. The post's spine is an eight-week self-trial with a defined pass/fail (two frozen lifts, log the top set, change nothing else, read the slope not the absolute gain), built on the non-responder literature that the sales pages omit entirely. Anchors, each stated with limits: Chilibeck et al., *Open Access J Sports Med* 2017 (22 RCTs, 721 participants, mean ages 57-70, +1.4 kg lean mass and greater chest/leg press — with the point made plainly that every trial had both arms lifting, so creatine is a multiplier and not a cause); Syrotuik & Bell 2004, *JSCR* (responders >20 vs non-responders <10 mmol/kg dry muscle after a 5-day load; 63% vs 40% type II fiber, and high baseline muscle creatine as the main driver); ISSN position stand 2017 (up to 30 g/day for 5 years, no kidney-function effect; monohydrate not bettered by any other form); Chilibeck et al., *MSSE* 2015 (12 months, postmenopausal women, 0.1 g/kg/day + 3x/week lifting — read precisely as preservation of femoral neck BMD plus improved bone *geometry*, not the "creatine builds bone" the whole SERP renders it as); Gordji-Nejad et al., *Sci Rep* 2024 (single 0.35 g/kg dose in acute sleep deprivation, ~5x a daily dose, flagged as not evidence for 5 g/day making you sharper at work). The water-weight section exists because that's the single most common reason people quit before saturation. Links out to `protein-intake-for-muscle-after-40`, `lose-fat-without-losing-muscle-after-40` (the scale confound), and `exercises-for-bone-density-after-40` (the bone section); inbound link added from the protein post's "no powders required" passage, which was the least link-dense relevant option at 4 outbound links and already framed food as upstream of purchases.

Small enough that all five can reasonably reference each other; no sub-grouping needed yet. **Where this cluster should grow next:** creatine was taken on 2026-09-03 (above). Alcohol and recovery remains the open candidate, though it's wellness-side rather than nutrition. Ruled out in the 2026-08-31 research: perimenopause training (SERP is genuinely strong, including Stanford Lifestyle Medicine, and it sits outside a trainer's most defensible lane) and evening-workout fueling (overlaps `meal-prep-for-busy-professionals`). Note for future audits: this is now the site's only supplement post, and it deliberately covers the priority-order argument (training, protein, sleep, then supplements) in its close — a second supplement post would need to clear that, not restate it.

### 5. Conditioning / aerobic fitness (2 posts — new cluster as of 2026-08-07)

- `cardio-fitness-test-at-home-after-40` (2026-08-07) — the seed post for a pillar the site had zero coverage of across its first 40 posts. Uses cluster 1's self-test format (test, score, decompose, fix) but measures conditioning rather than mobility or pain, which is why it sits here instead of in cluster 1. Three tests: a timed four-flight stair climb (ESC/EACVI 2020, Peteiro, 165 patients), one-minute heart rate recovery taken off that same climb (Cole et al., NEJM 1999, 2,468 adults, ≤12 bpm cutoff), and the YMCA three-minute step test scored against age/sex norms. The differentiator against the SERP is decomposition plus honesty about the research cohorts, since the ranking pages are split between VO2-max calculators that stop at a number and listicles with no scoring. Links out to `sitting-rising-test-at-home` (the other composite-score-needs-decomposing post), `sleep-and-muscle-recovery-for-strength-training-over-30` (heart rate recovery is sleep-sensitive), and `how-often-should-you-strength-train-after-30` (the fit-it-around-lifting section). Inbound link added from `what-to-do-on-rest-days-strength-training` at its 60-minute rest-day section, framed to preserve that post's distinction between easy recovery walks and actual aerobic training.

- `return-to-running-after-40-readiness-tests` (2026-08-28) — the second post in this cluster, taking the "distinct population" option flagged below (returning runners) rather than a new measurement. Tests musculoskeletal readiness for running load, which is why it complements the seed post rather than competing: that one measures the engine, this one measures the chassis, and the two are explicitly cross-linked on that framing. Three tests: single-leg calf raise capacity (Hébert-Losier et al. 2017, *Physiotherapy* — 566 adults aged 20-81, medians 24 men / 21 women, though on a 10° incline, so the post says plainly the numbers aren't interchangeable with a flat-floor test), single-leg hop tolerance (symmetry benchmark openly flagged as adapted from ACL return-to-sport literature, not validated for running), and a walk-jog rehearsal scored on the 48-hour delayed response. The organizing idea is the adaptation-rate mismatch: cardiovascular fitness returns in weeks, tendon and bone in months, so people increase load based on the fastest-adapting system they own and get caught around week four. SERP differentiator is that the entire consumer field (Nike, HIGH5, Runner's Blueprint, COOSPO, Midlife Running) only covers *pacing the increase* (10 percent rule, run-walk intervals) and never asks whether the starting dose is safe; the clinical readiness-criteria literature that does exist is written for physios. Has a dedicated "what these tests can't tell you" section, since the research explicitly states the calf raise test does not measure Achilles integrity, reactive strength, or injury risk. **Note for future audits:** knee-to-wall dorsiflexion was deliberately NOT made a fourth test — `ankle-mobility-test-at-home` fully owns it with its own pass/fail bands, so it's a cross-link instead. Single-leg squat was likewise avoided because `knee-pain-when-sitting-at-a-desk` already scores it as its Test 2. Links out to `ankle-mobility-test-at-home` and `cardio-fitness-test-at-home-after-40`; inbound link added from the seed post's easy-sessions paragraph, framed as gating running as the highest-impact option on its list.

Where this cluster should grow next: **retired the earlier "zone 2 programming for lifters" suggestion on 2026-08-10.** The SERP is genuinely weak there, but the seed post already covers talk-test pacing, two 30-40 minute easy sessions, the 4x4 protocol, a week-by-week build, and a full section on lifting order and same-day vs separate-day scheduling. A dedicated zone-2 post would clear 30% overlap, and a weak SERP doesn't rescue a rewrite. Cardio-versus-lifting scheduling has the same problem for the same reason. The rule that earned this cluster its second post still holds for its third: it needs either a genuinely new measurement or a distinct population, not another angle on aerobic programming. **The returning-runners population was taken on 2026-08-28** (above), which leaves cardio drift, resting heart rate trends, and the post-cardiac-event population as the open candidates.

## Linking philosophy

- **No blanket sibling links.** The automated "Related Articles" block (`getRelatedPosts` in `src/lib/blog.ts`) scores candidates by shared *words* (not whole tag phrases — tags here are unique long-tail keyword strings per post, so exact-tag matching finds almost nothing between clearly related posts) weighted by inverse document frequency, so specific overlap (e.g. "shoulder" + "impingement") counts far more than near-universal words ("desk", "San Jose"). It's automatic and doesn't need per-post maintenance, but it's a relevance signal, not a substitute for judgment — a hand-picked contextual link in prose is always stronger than an algorithmic card grid.
- **Manual inline links stay conservative:** one link where it earns its place, in prose, at a natural anchor. Not a "related posts" dump inside the body. (This is already the `/new-blog` Step 4 rule — this map is what tells you *which* post is the right one to link, before you get there.)
- **Every post should route toward `/tools/movement-screen` and `/#apply`** in its closing section regardless of cluster.

## Before writing a new post

1. Check which cluster (if any) the topic belongs to above.
2. If it's cluster 1 (desk-worker self-check), check the 30%-overlap rule against all 16 existing posts, not just the obvious neighbor.
3. Decide the 1-2 existing posts that should get an inbound link, and which existing post(s) the new one should link out to — based on the cluster map above, not a fresh search each time.
4. After publishing, add the new post to the relevant cluster list in this file (or start a new cluster section if it doesn't fit an existing one).
